## 1. What the document actually is

This is a **press release**, not the binding instrument. It says:

1. RBI issued **draft** CVA Directions on 7 Aug 2026 and took comments until 28 Aug.
2. The **draft** proposed four changes to the CVA-risk capital charge for commercial banks:
   - (i) letting eligible banks use a **simpler approach**;
   - (ii) more risk-sensitive **supervisory risk weights by counterparty sector and credit quality**;
   - (iii) clearer **eligibility and recognition of CVA hedges**;
   - (iv) **separate systematic and idiosyncratic** components in full BA-CVA, to handle imperfectly aligned indirect hedges.
3. Comments were incorporated, so the **final text may differ from the draft**. A statement on the feedback is in the Annex.
4. The binding text is the separately released *RBI (Commercial Banks – Credit Valuation Adjustment Framework) Directions, 2026*.
5. It is **effective 1 April 2027**.

The press release itself is the right **trigger** for the engine. The correct output is not a rule but a **readiness alert**:
- fetch the Directions and the Annex;
- note the effective date (5.5 months of lead time);
- route the alert to Treasury Risk, Capital Management and Finance.

## 3. What a bank actually does with the CVA Directions

**Context.** CVA risk is the risk of losses from changes in the credit spreads of derivative counterparties. RBI's framework follows the Basel III CVA standard (BCBS, revised 2017/2020). The parameters below are the Basel reference values; confirm each against the final RBI Directions and Annex.

**Applicability and approach choice (Board-level decision):**
- **Simpler approach:** likely the counterpart of Basel's option for banks below a materiality threshold of non-centrally-cleared derivatives, which may set CVA capital as a multiple of their counterparty-credit-risk capital. Check the RBI eligibility criteria.
- **BA-CVA, reduced version:** no hedge recognition; supervisory risk weight × EAD (SA-CCR) × effective maturity × discount factor, summed per counterparty.
- **BA-CVA, full version:** recognizes eligible single-name and index CDS hedges. Systematic and idiosyncratic components are separated (Basel uses a correlation of 0.5 and a floor of β = 0.25 on hedge benefit). This is draft revision (iv).
- **SA-CVA:** sensitivity-based and needs supervisory approval, if RBI permits it.

**Impacted perimeter:**

| Area | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| OTC derivatives book (FX forwards and swaps, IRS, CCS, options), by netting set | Treasury / Global Markets |
| SA-CCR EAD per netting set, effective maturity | Credit Risk (CCR), Risk IT |
| Counterparty sector and credit-quality (IG/HY) mapping for supervisory risk weights | Credit Risk, Ratings |
| CVA hedges (CDS) eligibility and recognition | Market Risk, Treasury |
| CVA capital charge → RWA → CRAR; ICAAP; capital plan | Finance / Capital Management, ALCO, Board |
| Regulatory returns and Pillar 3 disclosure | Regulatory Reporting |
| Policy and approach selection; supervisory notifications | Risk Committee, Compliance |

**Expected engine output for this press release:**
1. A readiness alert: *Final CVA Directions issued, effective 2027-04-01; binding text in the linked Directions and Annex*. The pipeline should fetch them.
2. A gap assessment against the bank's current CVA computation under the existing Basel III capital instructions.
3. A capital impact estimate: CVA RWA under each eligible approach, and the CRAR delta.
4. A dated plan: approach decision (Board) → data and systems build → parallel run → go-live 2027-04-01, with owners.
5. Penalty: "not applicable / supervisory". The real risk is a capital shortfall or misreporting, not a fine.
